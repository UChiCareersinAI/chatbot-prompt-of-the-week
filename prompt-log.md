# July 30

Adapt a Skill Someone Else Built

Have a useful skill, template, or workflow that was created for someone else? Upload the relevant files and use this prompt to adapt it to your own project.

My coworker created a skill file for building [XYZ], but their output looks different from what I need. Here are three files:

• [Skill file]

• [What it builds for them]

• [Example of what I need it to build for me]

Review the skill file that builds XYZ for them, the resulting file, and compare it to my goal. Suggest revisions to the skill file so it can produce the output shown in my example. Flag any issues or questions

Tip: Replace the bracketed text with details about your project. Do not upload confidential, private, or sensitive information.

# August 13

The Opportunity:

The last few weeks of an internship are just as important as the first. How you close out an experience shapes what you take away from it and how you're remembered!

The Output:

Chat with AI to generate a personalized end-of-internship action checklist, then use it to draft real outputs: feedback request emails, thank-you notes, and professional reflections you can carry forward.

The Prompt:

I am a [year] undergraduate at the University of Chicago studying [major(s)], wrapping up a [internship/research position] at [type of organization] in [City].

My role involved [specific description of your day-to-day work, key projects, who you worked with... the more detail you give, the more tailored your checklist will be]. I have approximately [X weeks] left.

Generate a personalized end-of-internship checklist that helps me close out this experience professionally, ask for meaningful feedback from my supervisor, and maintain the relationships I've built. Prioritize actions that will serve me in future job or internship searches.

When you're done, ask me which item I'd like to tackle first and offer to help me draft it.

# September 24

Fill Your Skill Gaps

The Opportunity: A job description tells you what an employer wants, but it can be harder to see which skills you already have and which ones you need to build.

The Output: Get a skills assessment based on your experience, followed by a practical plan for what to learn next.

# October 1

As technology and the job market continue to evolve rapidly, the future can be confusing. Many roles and job titles that have existed for years are disappearing, while new ones appear daily. This can make it tough to know what you're searching for when it comes to post-grad opportunities! That's why it can be more important than ever to really understand the specific interests, qualities, and skillsets that are uniquely you.

First, take 3-5 minutes to write down what you're passionate about. What do you enjoy doing? What do you do well? (Think through activities you've done growing up, awards or recognitions you've received, projects you've published).

Then, take it to a Chatbot for a conversation: "Here are the things I'm passionate about, what I enjoy doing, and what I do well [your list]. What patterns do you see? What do you think my superpowers are?" From there, continue the conversation- ask it what roles or career opportunities you should pursue as you continue to learn & grow, and how you can best position yourself and your superpowers to achieve them.

# October 8

The Opportunity: October is a great time to build your network before the Winter and Spring recruiting season, but a blank “Hi, I’d love to pick your brain” email rarely receives responses. Alumni and professionals are far more likely to reply to a short, specific message that shows you’ve done your homework.

The Output: Chat with AI to identify the types of people* worth talking to, then walk away with a short outreach message** and five questions for a 20-minute informational conversation.

The Prompt:

I am a [1st/2nd/3rd/4th] year undergraduate at the University of Chicago studying [major(s)], interested in [role, field, or “not sure yet”]. The strengths I’d point to are [2-3 things from your list last week].

Help me figure out who to talk to. Suggest 3 types of people (by job title, industry, or career path) whose work connects to my interests and strengths. For each, explain what I could learn from them.

Then pick the one I’m most excited about and draft: (1) a 3-4 sentence outreach message, and (2) five specific questions for a 20-minute conversation, none of which could be answered by Googling.

Ask me one clarifying question before you start if you need more context.

*Do not ask for specific names (AI may hallucinate). Instead, ask for types/titles, then look for people on LinkedIn or through the UChicago alumni directory.

**Edit the message so it sounds like you. Don’t paste in anyone’s private information.

# October 22

The Opportunity

There are lots of employer info sessions and networking events happening on campus this Fall. Don’t let the connections you make get lost during the busy midterm season! The people who speak at these events are the same people who read applications in January. Use the next four weeks to turn one session into a real connection.

The Output

A tailored follow-up message to a speaker or recruiter (under 100 words) and three specific questions to ask for a 15-minute chat. You’ll also get one skill from your background to mention so you’re remembered for something concrete.

The Prompt

I’m a [year] at UChicago studying [major], interested in [AI / tech / startup roles]. I just attended an info session for [Company] where [speaker name/title] talked about [one or two things you remember, e.g., their data team, a new product, how they hire interns]. Here’s my background in a few lines: [paste 3-5 resume bullets or projects].

Help me do three things:

1. Identify the one or two strengths from my background that best match what this company seems to value, and explain why.

2. Write a short, non-generic follow-up message (under 100 words) to send on LinkedIn or email within 48 hours. It should reference something specific from the session, mention one of my strengths, and ask for a 15-minute conversation.

3. Give me three questions to ask in that conversation that I couldn’t answer by reading their website.

Ask me any clarifying questions you need before you write anything. Keep the tone conversational, not formal or salesy.

# October 29

The Opportunity Application season is here, and most students have a class project, research assignment, or side build that they are proud of, but can’t explain well in two concise lines.

Recruiters skim a resume in seconds, and “Worked on a machine learning project” tells them nothing. Spend 10 minutes this week turning your best project into something memorable before deadlines arrive.

The Output -     Food for thought in developing two resume bullets (each under 25 words) that lead with impact. Be sure to rewrite these in your own voice.

- Template for a 30-second spoken version for when an interviewer says “tell me about a project” (use to write in your own voice)

- 2-3 examples of the detail most likely to prompt a good follow-up question.

The Prompt I’m a [year] at UChicago studying [major], applying for [AI / tech / startup] internships or roles. Here’s a project I want to showcase: [describe in detail: what you built or studied, tools you used, who it was for, why they wanted in the first place, and any results or numbers you remember]. I’m targeting roles like [job title or company type].

Ask me any clarifying questions you need before you write anything. Don’t exaggerate or invent results. If something is vague, flag it and ask me.

Help me do three things:

1. Provide 3 different examples of pairs of 2 resume bullet options highlighting this project. Each buttet should be under 25 words, start with a strong action verb, and include a specific result or scale (use a placeholder like [X%] if I haven’t given a number).

2. 2 examples of the outline for a 30-second spoken version I could use in an interview. 10% covering the problem and set up, 50% what I did, 40% impact/result.

3. Highlight 3 details in my project that may be the most interesting to an interviewer, and give me one tough follow-up question they might ask about each.

# November 5

The Opportunity: Thanksgiving break gives you a little bit of free time, and can be a great opportunity to connect with the people you’d want to talk to are often in town too (high school friends with shared professional goals, former teachers, local UChicago alumni). Requests sent the week of Thanksgiving often get buried, so use the prompt below to schedule conversations by Friday, Nov 13, with specific dates.

The Output: One or two people within reach of your break destination, a short scheduling message, and a backup plan.

The Prompt:

I’m a [year] studying [major], interested in [field]. I’m home in [city] Nov 23-28, and free [times]. People I can contact, by role/relationship: [e.g., a family friend in data science]. Pick the best one or two, draft a message for each offering two specific times, and suggest a backup if they can’t meet [e.g., If the week of 11/23 doesn't work, I'd happily meet over Zoom]. Ask one clarifying question before you start if you need it.

Tip: Rewrite the messages in your own voice. Don’t paste anyone’s private information, and keep the person’s name out of the prompt.

# November 12

The Opportunity: Recruiting season means a lot of open tabs: job postings, coffee chat follow-ups, a company someone mentioned in class, newsletters, lunch and learns, the list goes on! Instead of letting them pile up and slip through the cracks - connect Claude to Google Drive and keep one running tracker spreadsheet. Whenever you come across something interesting, drop in your notes or a link and let Claude log it for you.

The Output: A tracker spreadsheet in your Google Drive with tabs for applications and networking, which you can update in a sentence or two.

The Prompt (set up):

Create a spreadsheet in my Google Drive called “Job & Networking Tracker” with two tabs: Applications (Company, Role, Link, Deadline, Status, Next Step) and Networking (Name, Company, Role, How We Met, Last Contact, Follow-Up Date, Notes). Suggest any other columns that would help someone recruiting in [field]. Ask one clarifying question before you start if you need it.

The Prompt (each update):

Add this to my tracker: [paste a link or quick notes, e.g., “sustainability analyst role at a utility [link], due 12/1, want to ask my TA if she knows anyone there”]. Put it in the right tab, fill in what you can, and flag anything missing.

Tip: Check each entry Claude adds. Once a week, ask Claude “What’s due or needs a follow-up this week?” to turn your tracker into a to-do list.
